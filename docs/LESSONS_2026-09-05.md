# The preliminary of 2026-09-05: what was measured

[繁體中文](LESSONS_2026-09-05.zh-TW.md)

This file records what was measured in that round, not what follows from reading the
rules. The rules drawn from it are in `docs/COMPETITION_POLICY.md` and `docs/RUNBOOK.md`
(step 7); the reasons and the numbers are here.

Scenario: the organizer's preliminary problem script
(SHA-256 `1f378c65c849248bee9a5732b7ebb3777594dc1e50755842a831d24f7e4c731a`).
SC_Alien is on a 7100 km circular orbit, i = 120°, Ω = 30°; SC_Human on a 6800 km
circular orbit, i = 40°, Ω = 60°. The two planes are 84.3145° apart.
$T_A$ = 5953.858 s and $4T_A$ = 23815.434 s. Gravity is point mass only (Degree = 0,
Order = 0). The script has no ImpulsiveBurn and a single Propagate of 12000 s.

Announced coefficients: $k_t$ = 0.003982, $C_t$ = 6505.65, $k_v$ = 1.1862, $C_v$ = 9.0643.

## 1. The validator takes the completion time from the report's last row (worth 22.2266 points)

The trajectory we submitted entered the 5 km sphere at 5982.99 s. Under the policy of
the time, the script ran on to $4T_A$.

The organizer returned three numbers: C = 23815.43, D = 2.394, E = 6.996, and a total of
73.0203.

- D = 2.394 is the minimum distance they computed. It matches the smallest row of our
  report (t = 5982.992523 s, Δr = 2.394038 km, with 6.878 and 11.666 km on the rows
  before and after it) to three decimals. They saw the interception.
- C = 23815.43 is $4T_A$, the completion time of a mission that never intercepted, so
  the time term was 0.
- The only combination that gives the total is $50 + 0 + 23.0203 = 73.0203$.

So the validator took the completion time from the report's last row and the minimum
distance from all of its rows.

Rule §4 gives $T_{team} = \min(t_{int}, 4T_A)$, and the text allows both readings. That
is why reading the rule was not enough. §8 says 「所有結果以主辦單位驗證程式為準」
(every result is as the organizer's validator gives it).

Read as §4 intends, the score was $50 + 22.2266 + 23.0203 = 95.2470$.

### The fix, and its trap

`policy.mission_end_policy` defaults to `"interception"`: the Mission Sequence of a
successful candidate ends on the first row inside the sphere. A failed candidate still
runs to $4T_A$ (its $T_{team}$ is then $4T_A$ and its distance term is $\Delta r(4T_A)$,
worth at most 50 points). The stop has since moved deeper: it is where GMAT measures the
trajectory first `policy.stop_depth_km` (1.5 km by default) inside the sphere.

The length of the last segment has to be computed, not guessed. GMAT writes a row every
`Propagator.MaxStep` seconds counted from the **last maneuver**, so the phase of the rows
has nothing to do with our analytical $t_{int}$:

$$t_{\text{entry row}} = t_{\text{last burn}} + \left\lceil \frac{t_{int} - t_{\text{last burn}}}{\text{step}} \right\rceil \times \text{step}$$

Measured on a 6-burn candidate: $t_{int}$ = 5676.2438, last burn at 2556.7897,
step = 1 s. The entry row falls at 5676.7897, 0.546 s late. That trajectory passes at
12.6 km/s and has one row inside the sphere.

| Last segment | Last row | Δr | Validator reads |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Fixed 1.0 s | 0.45 s after the entry row | 7.57 km (outside) | 98.3151 |
| Fixed 2.0 s | 1.45 s after the entry row | 20.13 km (outside) | 98.3117 |
| Aligned to the entry row | The entry row | 1.86 km | 98.3167 |

With the stop on the entry row, both readings give the same score.
`scripts/check_report_rows.py` caught this bug in its own author's fix.

## 2. The organizer's own scoring fixes the units

The rules did not print the input units. The organizer returned E = 6.996 and a total of
73.0203:

$$\frac{25}{1+e^{1.1862(6.996-9.0643)}} = 23.0203, \qquad 50 + 0 + 23.0203 = 73.0203$$

Read in m/s, $C_v$ would overflow the exponent, the term would be 0 and the total
50.0000. **ΔV is in km/s and time in s.** Time in seconds also fits the rest:
$C_t$ = 6505.65 lies inside the 23815 s window, and C came back as 23815.43 with no
conversion.

## 3. The announced coefficients reverse the "least ΔV" default

With these coefficients the time term falls off a cliff at 6505 s and is down to 0.47 points
at 7500 s, while the ΔV term still gives 24 points at 6 km/s. Burns were nearly free, and
time decided the score.

The search layer's built-in default does the opposite: least ΔV, one or two burns.

| | Interception | Total ΔV | Score |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Best of the built-in search | 21558 s | 1.557 km/s | 74.997 |
| Burn count and spacing left free | 5676 s | 6.184 km/s | **98.319** |

### The search order that worked

1. Compute $\partial S/\partial t_{int}$ and $\partial S/\partial \Delta V$ to see which
   term is the lever.
2. Run `scripts/safe_delta_v_floor.py` to map the Earth-safe single-burn ΔV against
   arrival time. **That column is an upper bound, not a floor.** The table asks
   8.34 km/s at 5700 s; the five-burn solution that flew arrived at 5675 s for
   6.19 km/s, 2.15 km/s less. Use the table to find the reachable window and to budget
   the number of burns. It is not the answer.
3. Split a single burn over the limit into N burns: the first N−1 at the limit along one
   direction, the last one completed by Lambert. The organizer's example solution has
   this structure (1.5 + 0.741 km/s).
4. Refine with the spacing free. With the gaps no longer locked at the minimum the score
   rose by 3 points. The best structure was one burn, a 2264 s coast, then a burst of
   burns, not burns at even intervals.
5. Make every constraint a **hard wall**, not a penalty. A refinement with soft
   penalties returned `|dv| = 1495.098 m/s`, over the 1495 m/s cap, and a solution
   sitting on the altitude floor.

## 4. The central-body check has to cover the whole arc

Point-mass gravity has no surface. The integrator will carry a spacecraft through the
center of the Earth and report an interception on the other side.

- Every cheap Lambert arc goes below the surface before or after the interception. The
  single-burn minima at 3575 s (perigee 3931 km), 6505 s (4198 km) and 9444 s (5475 km)
  all go below it before the interception.
- With the wall only up to the interception, the optimizer trades a dive for speed: a
  95.25-point solution hit the ground 100 s after the interception.
- `constraints.flies_through_central_body` looks only up to the interception. It is a
  gate before submission, not a search constraint. The search has to carry its own wall,
  and `scripts/audit_burn_table.py --require-safe-through-horizon` checks afterwards.
  (When the force model is not two-body, the audit judges up to $T_{max}$ without the
  flag and leaves the interception to the GMAT report.)

Under the strict requirement that nothing goes below the surface over all of $4T_A$, the
best was 7 burns, 9.700 km/s, interception at 4671.5 s, **82.98 points**.

## 5. Do not cut safety margins for the third decimal

We pushed every burn from 1495 to 1499.999 m/s and every gap from 101 to 100.0001 s:

| | Score | Closest approach | Margin to a violation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| On the boundary | 98.3202 | 4.15 **km** | 1 m/s / 0.1 ms |
| Margins kept | 98.3185 | 4.15 **m** | 5 m/s / 1 s |

That gained 0.0016 points for a fifth of the impulse margin, while each violating maneuver
costs 10 points and the validator's floating-point tolerance was never announced. The
first tie-break of §6 is the minimum relative distance, so at nearly the same total the
version with its margins kept wins the tie.

## 6. What this round reached, and the provable upper bound

A relaxed upper bound from the score formula alone:

$$S \le 50 + \frac{25}{1+e^{-k_t C_t}} + \frac{25}{1+e^{-k_v C_v}} = 99.9994650$$

It takes $t=0$ and $\Delta V=0$, which no flight reaches: the craft start 12842 km apart,
and an impulsive burn does not change a position.

Reached, and confirmed by a replay in real GMAT R2026a: **98.3185410** (6 burns, total ΔV
6.184 km/s, interception at 5676.24 s, closest approach **4.15 m**, altitude before the
interception 422 km). 66 jobs over 5, 6 and 7 burns from 37 starts all converged to one
family. Seven burns gained nothing, because the ΔV term is close to saturated near
6 km/s. The 1.68 points left to the bound are the cost of planes 84.31° apart; more
search did not move them.

## 7. An open template

This round's Mission Sequence has one Propagate and no `ImpulsiveBurn` or `Maneuver`.
The rules say B 「可進行多次瞬時脈衝機動」 (may make several impulsive maneuvers), and the
template gives no way to write one. Adding the resources and commands is what the problem
asks for, so it is not a forbidden edit.

- `script_renderer._render_open_template` takes this path, and
  `tests/unit/test_open_template.py` covers it.
- `pursuer_name` is required here. With no `Maneuver` command the parser cannot tell
  which craft is ours, and it fails closed. `target_name` may be left out; the parser
  takes the other craft.
- The team-number placeholder in `ReportFile1.Filename` must be replaced with the team
  number. The organizer requires it.

## 8. Stock GmatConsole does not run a script with OpenFrames

The organizer's script has `OpenFramesInterface`, `OpenFramesView` and `GroundTrack`.
Console mode skips the GUI plugins by a hard-coded check (the string is in
`libGmatBase.so`), and the whole script is refused: 58 errors and exit 1, measured.

- `orbitwin solve --gmat-verify-top N` is not affected, because the diagnostic copy
  comments out the plot objects.
- The exact replays of `orbitwin baseline` and `certify` **fail**. Since 2026-11-06
  (signed off in `docs/COMPETITION_POLICY.md`), when `gmat.console_copy` is on, as in the
  ranking profiles, they fly a copy that leaves out only the plot lines and is proven
  line by line against the submitted script (`docs/RUNBOOK.md` step 6c).
- Without that copy, the official report can only come from the GMAT **GUI**
  (`docs/RUNBOOK.md` step 6b). Then run `check_report_rows` as in RUNBOOK step 7.
- The error message reads only GMAT's own errors: the `**** ERROR ****` records, and the
  `... Exception:` lines GMAT prints without that mark. Each is quoted once, with the
  line GMAT printed beside it. An error counts against the OpenFrames objects when that
  line is the Create or a field of an OpenFrames object the script creates. Only when
  GMAT printed no line does the R2026a wording decide.
  An error about anything else is quoted first (`GMAT reported: ...`), and the message
  then does not send anyone to the GUI: the GUI uses the same interpreter and refuses
  it too, and the official script cannot be changed. The run stops and the problem goes
  to the organizer (RUNBOOK, Stop rules). The message does not send anyone to the GUI
  either when there are more errors than it reads (over 10 000 distinct ones).
  GroundTrack is named only in the case that was measured: the missing report is one
  the script writes (WriteReport is not false), and the GroundTrack is created before
  that ReportFile. A timeout or a file-size limit is not blamed on the plot objects.
